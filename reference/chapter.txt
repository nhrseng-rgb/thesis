3.2. Research Design

         The study will employ a quantitative, simulation-based optimization design with a staged
screening    approach.     The   research    design    systematically     isolates   the   effects  of   site spacing,
electrical  dispersion,     and  renewable      energy   (RE)    penetration    by   holding   total  GFM     capacity
constant during spacing tests and establishing pre-retirement baselines for RE scenarios.

         This study will be anchored on the principle that the replacement ratio (ρ) is not constant
but  a  variable    that   depends    on:   (1)   ancillary   service    constraints    (frequency     nadir,  RoCoF,
regulation   reserve,   and   reactive power      capability);   (2) displacement of synchronous generators
(SG);   (3)   RE   penetration,     which    affects   the   net  load   ramps     and   baseline    inertia;  (4)  site
configuration,    meaning the electrical        spacing   and   dispersion    of the Multi-BESS units; and (5)
system stability requirements, namely small-signal damping (eigenvalue stability), transient rotor
angle   stability,  and  system    strength    as  measured     by the SCR      and gSCR,      which can require       a
higher   ratio than the ancillary service constraints alone. The output is a ratio surface, annotated
with the binding constraint at each point.

         The   study   will  not   be  a  case  study    of the   Philippine    grid;  however, it    will  inherit   its
characteristics. The Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao grids are coupled by HVDC links, so they are
asynchronous. Each grid is therefore modeled as a frequency island in which frequency support
is provided    only   by   its local  SGs    and   GFM     units.  An   HVDC      link  supplies    scheduled active
power,    not   synchronous      inertia.   The    chain   is  Luzon(source),       Visayas    (first  receiver),   and
Mindanao (second receiver). For simplicity, the characteristics of Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao
will be respectively, Grid A, Grid B, Grid C.

         The   assessment follows       a  fixed  sequence:     (i) the  regulating-reserve assessment, (ii) the
contingency     (N-1)assessment       of  each   configuration, (iii)     the  reactive-power and intermittency
assessment, (iv) the stability assessment (small-signal damping, transient rotor angle, gSCR), and
(v) the spatial-distribution step, which examines how site count and dispersion change the result
when total GFM capacity is fixed.

3.3 Research Instruments

         Because this study uses a simulation-based research design, its research instruments will
consist   of  software    tools   for  network     modeling,     computational      analysis,   optimization,     and
validation. The proposed software environment comprises the following:

(1) pandapower version 3.5.6 will be used to construct and calibrate the three IEEE 39-bus grid
proxies,   perform    steady-state   power-flow      and   short-circuit   calculations,   screen   eligible BESS

also   prepare      network      data    for   MATLAB          and    verify    capacity     consistency      during
matched-capacity      spacing    tests. Candidate-site     screening    will  identify   eligible  locations;   final
BESS placement and sizing will be determined through the optimization procedures.

(2) Visual    Studio   Code    version    1.140 will serve as the development environment for writing,
organizing, and     debugging the study’s       scripts.  These    scripts  will  coordinate Python–MATLAB
data  exchange,    configure experimental cases, manage batch execution, and organize simulation
outputs and reproducibility records.

(3)  MATLAB        and    Simulink     Release    R2026b      will  be  used   for  RMS    dynamic     simulations,
state-space modeling, eigenvalue and damping analysis, and detailed switching-model validation.

         RE   (PV)   is  modeled     as  non-synchronous       grid-following injection,       distributed    over  the
load buses in proportion to load. The RE penetration is

         The   Grid-Forming       (GFM)     BESS     is held   idle  at 0  MW     active   power    at  the pre-event
operating point. This is a deliberate modeling choice to ensure that the GFM-BESS is evaluated
purely as a contingency reserve device, rather than as a steady-state energy source. Although it is
injecting   0  MW,     the   GFM     inverters    remain    online   to  establish    the  voltage    and   frequency
reference.   By keeping      the  active  power output      at  zero,  100% of     the GFM      BESS active power
capability    and    energy    buffer    are   available    to   provide    Fast   Frequency       Response      (FFR)
immediately following the N-1 contingency.

         To isolate the impact of generator retirement, a control baseline is first established at RE
penetrations    of 20%,    40%, 60%, and 80% with 100% of the synchronous generation (SG) fleet
retained.   Because    the  RE    is modeled     as  non-synchronous        Grid-Following       (GFL)    injection, it
provides no physical inertia to the grid. Therefore, to maintain stability during this baseline, the
SGs are redispatched: their active power output is reduced to accommodate the GFL renewable
generation,    but the   units remain physically online and spinning to provide synchronous inertia.
This baseline serves as a scientific control. It captures the frequency response degradation caused
solely  by GFL renewables         displacing    SG active power. When synchronous generators are later
retired, the additional     degradation reflects the combined effect of lost inertia, reduced governor
headroom,     changes     in  the  size  of   the  largest  online   unit,  and   reduced    system    strength.   The
reported   replacement      ratio  therefore    captures    the  total  BESS     capacity    needed    to  restore  the
frequency    response to acceptable limits under combined RE and displacement stress, not solely
the inertia-replacement component.
